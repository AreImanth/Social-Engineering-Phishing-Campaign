🎣 You just experienced a simulated phishing attempt

This page is part of a security-awareness training exercise. The login page you just saw was fake — nothing you typed was recorded, stored, or sent anywhere. Here's what should have tipped you off:

Red flags on that page

  • The URL didn't match the real service. Look at the address bar shown at the top of the page (e.g. ...-secure-login.example-training.local) — a real login page's domain will exactly match the company's actual domain.
  • Urgency and fear language. Phrases like "unusual activity detected," "account will be suspended," or "storage full" are designed to make you act before you think.
  • Generic or slightly-off branding. Colors and logos that are "close enough" but not quite right are a common tell.
  • Unsolicited link in an email or message. Real companies rarely ask you to "verify your account" via a link — they'll usually direct you to open the app or type the address yourself.
  • Requests for credentials outside a normal flow. If you weren't actively trying to log in somewhere, a sudden login prompt is suspicious.
What to do next time: Don't click the link. Instead, open a new browser tab and navigate to the site directly, or open the company's official app, to check your account status.
Good news: Nothing you entered on the previous page was ever transmitted or stored. This exercise only recorded that you reached this page — no username, password, or personal data was captured at any point.

Report suspicious emails

In a real workplace, use your organization's "Report Phishing" button or forward suspicious emails to your IT/security team instead of clicking links or replying.
